📈 Key Exploratory Data Analysis Takeaways

1. Target Skewness (+1.88)

Sale prices cluster between $120k and $220k with a long luxury right tail up to $755k. Natural log transformation reduces skewness to +0.12.

2. Overall Quality (r = +0.791)

Overall Quality rating is the single most dominant non-spatial driver. Ratings 9 and 10 trigger exponential pricing jumps.

3. Engineered TotalSF (r = +0.782)

Combining Basement, 1st Floor, and 2nd Floor square footage created the strongest unified physical dimension in the dataset.

4. De Cock Outlier Rule

Dean De Cock explicitly recommended removing partial estate sales >4,000 sq ft sold under $300k to eliminate destructive leverage.

🎓 Academic Viva Voce Preparation Guide
Q1: Why did you choose Ames Housing over Boston Housing? ▼
The Boston Housing dataset is officially deprecated in Scikit-Learn due to ethical concerns and a small sample size (506 records, 13 variables). The Ames Housing dataset provides 1,460 genuine sales, 81 comprehensive variables, and reflects modern residential transactions.
Q2: How was data leakage prevented during preprocessing? ▼
The 80% train / 20% test partition was executed prior to computing any imputation or scaling. The median imputation statistics and category encodings were fitted strictly on the training partition and transformed onto the test set without parameter re-estimation.
Q3: Why did Gradient Boosting outperform the Decision Tree? ▼
A single decision tree recursively partitions data until leaves are pure, causing high variance and memorization of training noise (Train R² = 0.981 vs CV R² = 0.749). Gradient Boosting builds shallow trees sequentially with shrinkage (learning rate = 0.08) to optimize residuals without overfitting.
